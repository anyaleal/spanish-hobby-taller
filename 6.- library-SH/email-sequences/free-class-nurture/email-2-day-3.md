I promised you a real pattern. Here's one you've almost certainly been getting backwards, and it isn't your fault.

You learned that *me gusta el café* means "I like coffee." Reasonable. Then *me gustan los libros* shows up, the verb quietly grows an ‑n, and nobody tells you why. If *gustar* means "to like," why is it agreeing with the books instead of with you?

Because *gustar* doesn't mean "to like."

It means "to be pleasing." Flip the sentence and watch it click: *me gusta el café* is really "coffee is pleasing to me." *Me gustan los libros* is "books are pleasing to me." The books are the ones doing the pleasing, so the verb agrees with *them*, not with you. Nothing to memorize. The grammar was logical the whole time. You were just handed the wrong translation.

Here's the part that makes this worth your evening: it isn't one verb. It's a whole family running on the exact same machine.

- *Me encanta* → it enchants me
- *Me importa* → it matters to me
- *Me duele* → it hurts me
- *Me falta* → it's lacking to me

Learn the shape once and a dozen "irregular" sentences stop being irregular. That's the whole idea behind Spanish Hobby: you memorize less because you finally see the machine underneath.
