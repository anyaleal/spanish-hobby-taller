By now a fair question might be forming, and I'd rather you ask it out loud than let it sit: is this actually different, or is it the same information you've already tried, just repackaged? And underneath that one, quieter, the real one: will you start this and quit again, the way you did with the apps and the courses before it?

Ask both. You should.

Here's the honest difference, and it isn't that I have more content. Apps and courses push information at you, a fixed path you're meant to absorb in order, and the work of turning it into real understanding is left entirely to you. A class runs the other direction: you bring the confusion, the sentence you couldn't finish, the rule that never stuck, and we work backward from it to the pattern underneath, so the material is built around your Spanish instead of aimed at the average learner. Same language, opposite direction.

And the quitting. I don't think you walked away last time because you're someone who quits, I think you walked away because apps are quietly built to be quit, nobody notices when you stop, the streak is the only thread holding you, and the moment it snaps there's nothing real on the other side to return to. Leaving was the rational response to something that was never actually adapting to you.

One to one changes that math. A person notices, the class meets you where you actually are instead of where a curriculum assumes you should be, and progress becomes something you can feel, that half-second lag closing a little each time, which is a very different thing to come back to than a broken streak.
