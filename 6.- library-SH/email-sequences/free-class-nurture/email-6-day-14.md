You know what the classes are now and why they work, so let me make it concrete and walk you through what a first one actually feels like, from start to finish.

You arrive a little unsure, which nearly everyone does. There's no textbook open and no quiz waiting, just a sentence you tried to say last week that came out wrong and has been quietly bothering you since. Say you never quite know whether it's *por* or *para*, and you've been guessing your way past it for years.

We start right there, from your Spanish rather than from page one of anything. You say the sentence, we find the exact spot where it broke, and instead of me handing you the "correct" word to memorize, we go underneath it: *para* points forward, to a destination or a purpose, the aim ahead, while *por* points back, to a cause, a reason, the motive behind. One is where you're headed and the other is why you set off. Suddenly it isn't two words to guess between, it's a direction you can feel.

Then you try it yourself, live, in a sentence you build from scratch, and it holds. That's the moment I'm actually there for, the small quiet click, the *ah, that's why*. And it's rarely just the one sentence, because the same idea reaches back and quietly settles three or four other things you'd been getting wrong without ever knowing they were related.

You leave with a pattern, not a homework list. There's nothing to cram before next time, because understanding doesn't need re-reviewing the way memorizing does, and the next class starts from the real you instead of lesson twelve of a course that never met you.

That's one class. The first two are free, and the click is the one part no email can hand you.
