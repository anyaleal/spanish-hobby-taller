You've probably hit the place where progress quietly stops: you can follow a podcast but freeze the moment it's your turn to speak, you read far faster than you talk, and somewhere in the middle of every sentence you're still translating, English first and Spanish second, always half a second behind. It's a lonely place to be stuck.

The usual advice is to study harder: more words, more flashcards, another app.

Here's why that keeps letting you down. Memorizing hands you more pieces, but it never hands you the machine that assembles them, so you can know three thousand words and still stall the moment you open your mouth, because speaking isn't retrieval, it's assembly, in real time and under a little pressure. Nobody ever taught you the assembly rules, so your brain does the only thing it can, building each sentence in English and carrying it across. That half-second lag is the ceiling.

The relief is this: you're not stuck because you're bad at languages, and not because you didn't try hard enough. You tried plenty. You're stuck because a layer is missing, the one that connects the pieces you already have, and that layer is learnable, mostly a handful of patterns, so once you see them the plateau stops behaving like a wall.

This is exactly where one to one earns its place. A book teaches the average learner and an app walks everyone through the same sequence, but your plateau is specific, built from your own confusions and the two or three mistakes you keep making, so in a class I find those and rebuild them with you, until the structure fits your head instead of a generic curriculum.

The first two classes are free, and that's enough to feel the lag start to close.
