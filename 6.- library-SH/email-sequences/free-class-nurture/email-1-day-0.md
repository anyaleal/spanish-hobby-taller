You downloaded The Spanish Map, which means you already suspect the thing most learners never get told: Spanish isn't a pile of words to memorize. It has a logic. A shape. Once you see it, ten things you thought were unrelated turn out to be the same idea.

That's what the map gives you. The terrain, seen from above.

Here's the one thing a map can't do. It can't notice the exact moment you get lost. It can't see that you understand *ser* and *estar* perfectly on paper, then freeze the second you actually have to choose one out loud. A map shows the whole country at once. It can't walk a single road with you, at your pace, and point at the thing confusing you *right now*.

That part is the one I do.

I teach Spanish one to one. Not by drilling vocabulary, but by finding the pattern underneath whatever is tripping you up, so you understand it once instead of memorizing it fifty times.

Over the next couple of weeks I'll show you how that actually works: a real pattern or two, why memorizing keeps letting you down, and what changes when someone explains the *why* while you're the one asking the questions.

And when you're curious to feel it yourself, the first two classes are free. Two, not one, because one is a taste and two is enough to genuinely test whether this method fits the way your mind works. No card, no commitment. You pick a time that fits your week.
